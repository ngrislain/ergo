# Coupled partitioned static stability

Implemented in **unreleased source after v0.5.34**, on native and WASM. This
extends the same fixed-pose, nominal-contact/connector model; it is not elasticity,
settling, earthquake simulation or a real-ABS strength guarantee.

## One physical model, bounded local solves

The existing validator first checks geometry, registered attachments, source
capacities and complete affected-domain membership. Independent contact/joint
islands remain independent. Each unanchored island first tries constructive
compression; when it needs general equilibrium, the same local SOCP kernel is
used for either one partition or a coupled set of computational partitions.

`equilibrium::certify` automatically partitions islands exceeding 256 bodies or
the local KKT dimension budget. Body centres are sorted in a deterministic
Morton (octree) order, with stable body IDs breaking ties, and cores are packed
against the complete incident contact/joint workload. No geometry, force edge or
mass is omitted. A core includes each owned body exactly once. Outside endpoint records
are borrowed for their identity/geometry but receive **no equilibrium rows and no
invented support or anchor** in that local problem.

Each crossing joint has copies of its six wrench components at the actual
registered world-space anchor. Each crossing contact has copies of the four
nonnegative corner-normal reactions on its real patch. Variable orientation is
canonical: both local copies describe the same reaction, and the kernel applies
opposite signs to the two endpoints. Consequently consensus enforces physical
action/reaction without dropping the moments generated by a force offset.

The kernel retains force, axial, bending and friction-torque limits; a joint with
no capacity carries no load beyond the declared floating tolerance. Every contact,
ground reaction and anchor is counted once physically. The quadratic objective
weights duplicated terms by one half so it is not doubled at a cut.

## Two-block consensus iteration

`equilibrium_partitioned.rs` coordinates two mathematical blocks:

1. All local subproblems minimize their physical quadratic objective plus an
   augmented-Lagrangian penalty towards the preceding sweep's interface state.
   They execute sequentially, so local matrices/factors are released before the
   next partition is built. This is not multi-block Gauss–Seidel.
2. The physical witness averages the two local reaction copies. Its physical
   feasible set is convex; an exact average of admissible copies remains in it,
   and the independent checker still catches numerical constraint errors. The
   consensus/dual iteration uses overrelaxation 1.8, separate from this physical
   average; its extrapolated target need not lie in a physical cone. Dual copies
   are numerically centred to retain their zero-sum subspace.

Force/torque penalties use mean body weight for contacts, and joint capacities
clamped to that weight and its 0.008 m stud-moment scale, not guessed material
stiffness. Local balance rows take moments about each body's own centre, while
the independent verifier retains its separate island-wide reference frame. The
kernel uses dimensionless reaction variables, a positive common objective rescale
and cone-preserving row normalization; recovered SI
reactions still face the unchanged independent physical checks. A small quadratic
regularization (coefficient 0.01 in those dimensionless units) of every reaction
removes near-free internal load-loop directions when capacities are effectively
unbreakable. Every partition uses the same canonical-island units. Every 20 sweeps residual balancing may
rescale the penalty and duals. Geometry, anchors, capacities and contact topology
never change during a solve.

After each sweep the **one assembled physical witness** is checked independently.
The solver may stop as soon as that witness passes. It does not claim that this
is the unique or minimum-utilisation solution, nor that every local optimizer or
dual sequence has reached its own optimality tolerance. A local solver status is
not a certificate: local candidates are also checked on their owned bodies.

## Bounded matrix-free balance correction

At sweeps 20, 100, 200 and 400 the coordinator may correct the candidate's balance
residuals with Jacobi-preconditioned conjugate gradients on `B Q Bᵀ`. `B` applies
body-centred force/moment equations in dimensionless units; `Q` removes joint
directions whose canonical capacity is zero and excludes inactive contact/ground
normals. Both products are matrix-free. There is no whole-island sparse matrix,
KKT assembly or factorization in this step.

Each attempt has at most four active-set passes of 1,024 iterations. A negative
normal is fixed at zero before another pass; clipping does not itself certify
equilibrium. The numeric-buffer/body-index workspace estimate is capped at
32 MiB (five six-component body-row vectors, one scalar-column scratch vector,
an active mask and an estimated body-ID index); an oversized correction is
skipped, without changing ADMM limits or
allocating a larger solver. This is not a whole-process RSS guarantee.

Correction can spend joint capacity differently from the local optimizers. Its
candidate may exceed a positive physical capacity or fail to balance after a
bounded active-set pass. Therefore **every corrected candidate passes the same
independent verifier**, including all positive capacities, zero-capacity axes,
normal signs and actual application points. Failure resumes the coupled local
iteration, not publication. No physical support, anchor or mass is added or
removed. Correction iteration counts are reported, not treated as certificates.

## Paged coordinator state

The coordinator has one `SolverWorkspace` interface. `MemoryWorkspace` is the
default for local/WASM entry points; authoritative shared-native validation uses
the transaction-backed implementation in `shared/equilibrium_workspace.rs`.
`certify_with_workspace`, `certify_partitioned_with_workspace` and
`validate_static_with_workspace` select storage, **not a different physical gate**.

The planner builds one bounded core/halo record at a time from canonical incident
edges and writes it to the workspace. It no longer retains every full plan and
boundary-dual vector simultaneously. Cores, owner/incident indexes and canonical
input geometry still materialize during planning. Plan records are checked for
dimensions, endpoint/source identities and local column/KKT bounds when loaded.
They never supply fictitious anchors or override the canonical problem.

The former full-size consensus, witness, sum, scaling and ownership arrays are
now reaction-column records. Native storage uses 256-column pages and at most
32 decoded pages (320 KiB of scalar records, plus page/container overhead), rather
than a scene-sized scalar vector. Four finite f64 values are encoded losslessly
as little-endian bytes with an ownership byte and reserved padding. SHA-256 binds
each page to its index and data. Missing **previously written** pages, invalid
records and checksum failures refuse the solve; they cannot become fresh zero
pages. New zero pages exist only before that page has ever been written.

Plans and scalar pages share the transaction's existing scratch disk/cache quota.
Dirty pages spill on eviction; only the active local plan is decoded at once.
Storage errors are inconclusive and there is no fallback to `MemoryWorkspace`.
This exchanges disk I/O for lower retained coordinator memory, not a process-RSS
guarantee or a claim that every small problem physically writes scalar pages.

The independent checker reads the complete canonical witness through
`ReactionRead`; bounded dense SOCP candidates use the same checker through a slice
adapter. Matrix-free correction reads/writes that witness directly through
`ReactionWrite`, retaining only its existing explicitly bounded scratch vectors.
Neither phase copies the complete paged witness into a hidden dense input vector.

## Independent body-at-a-time checking

`equilibrium_verify.rs` derives a compact incidence index from the canonical
problem, not from the partition plans. It checks every joint capacity and every
contact/ground normal reaction, then accumulates gravity and incident wrenches
**one body at a time**, using the real application points. There is no
whole-scene force/moment accumulator array. The independently constructed incidence
index still resides in memory; native witness values are fetched from the paged
workspace, while the browser/local default keeps its records resident.

Accumulation uses compensated summation and a conservative floating roundoff
allowance. Catastrophic cancellation cannot hide a body's small weight behind
huge opposing reactions; an exhausted numerical allowance is inconclusive.
Nonfinite data/coefficient/witness overflow is refused. Body residual tolerances
are based on the original physical island's weight and the same reference frame,
not loosened for a greater number of computational cuts.

Local passes alone, small iterate changes, or plausible total wall weights never
authorize publication. All canonical body balances, interfaces and capacities
must pass on one consistent reaction vector. The existing server still checks
permissions, quota and transaction predicates before the one atomic head CAS.

## Work limits and inconclusive results

Default targets/budgets are:

- 256 core bodies per initial partition;
- 2,048 local bodies including endpoint halos;
- 32,768 local scalar reaction columns, additionally constrained by the stronger
  KKT dimension limit below;
- at most 2,048 in the conservative local KKT dimension estimate
  `columns + 6*core + 14*joints + 4*contacts + supports`, before presolve;
- 1,000,000 scalar columns in the global reaction witness;
- 500 consensus sweeps, with an explicit Rust diagnostic maximum of 2,000;
- 20,000 local solves per island, independent of the sweep limit.

Morton-ordered cores are packed greedily against their actual incident
joint/contact/halo workload, stopping at a local budget or the core-body target.
A high-degree body does not shrink all unrelated cores. A body whose incident
boundary is itself too large is refused. KKT dimension bounds protect against dense factor fill, not merely input
nonzero counts; they are not a measured or guaranteed whole-process RSS quota.
There is **no unbounded whole-island fallback** after the partitioned path fails.

Local joint capacity cones additionally limit the numerical search to 10,000 mean
body-weight units for force and 10,000 mean-weight × 0.008 m units for moment,
or the original capacity if smaller. This is a stricter numerical search budget,
not a material-strength estimate. High-mechanical-advantage configurations may
therefore remain inconclusive even when physically feasible. Original capacities
are never increased, and final verification always uses the canonical problem.

Budget exhaustion, numerical conditioning, local solve failure without a checked
candidate, or nonconvergence returns typed `equilibrium::Inconclusive`. This says
**not certified**, not that the arrangement is physically unstable. The REST error
has HTTP 422, `validation_state:"inconclusive"`, `published:false`, and no candidate
revision: validation stopped before publication. It is not an uncertain write and
does not trigger an automatic mutation retry. Local native/WASM diagnostics
likewise expose `validation_state` and do not label an inconclusive result static.

## Reports and current limits

`ValidationReport.equilibrium_solver` records partition count, sweep count,
local-solve count and matrix-free balance-correction iteration count,
`spilled_partition_plans` / `spilled_reaction_columns`,
largest local body count and conservative KKT dimension, plus maximum scalar
interface force and joint-moment discrepancies at acceptance. Contact-induced
moments are checked by the body verifier, not summarised by the latter scalar
field. These interface diagnostics describe the last local SOCP copies **before
any balance correction**; they are not admission thresholds or residuals of the
accepted corrected witness. That witness has one canonical reaction per interface
and must independently pass every body balance. Across independent islands,
counts sum and maxima aggregate; sweep count
is the maximum. Compression-only and historical/unvalidated reports default
these diagnostics to zero (unavailable/no general solve).
The spill fields count logical records assigned to a spilling backend, including
records currently in its bounded dirty-page cache. They are not physical I/O-byte
counts or proof of a RAM saving for a problem that already fits in that cache.

This implements coupled local factorization and body-wise witness checking,
**not fully out-of-core validation**. The caller's physical scene, contact/joint
input, planner owner/incident indexes and independent witness incidence still
materialize. Native partition/interface records and reaction state now spill;
the browser/local default retains its workspace records in memory.
[Native transaction/authoring state](native-working-state.md) now spills staged
objects, predicates, edit journals and dependency traversal/index data. Assembly
rules and cached mesh/spatial data still materialize. The native piece cache is
disk-backed, but the complete physical scene is still built for validation.
Commit-record lists now use
[file-backed iteration](commit-history.md#bounded-native-commit-records), though
conditional upload retains a capped encoded buffer. Durable solver
checkpoints, persisted force-witness reuse, fully out-of-core physical inputs and
asynchronous publication jobs remain future work.
Do not infer that a large castle save now fits a fixed process RAM budget from
the local solver limits alone.
